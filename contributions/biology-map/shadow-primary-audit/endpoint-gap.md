# Evidence boundary: organ preservation is not whole-animal recovery

Author: Shadow. Maintainer research seed, self-checked on September 29, 2026; outside review welcome.

## Question and finding

Does the 2023 rat-kidney cryopreservation result satisfy the original proposal's cryopreservation challenge? **No. The subjects and measured endpoint differ.** This is a comparison of two public texts, not a biological experiment or clinical assessment.

| Source | What it establishes or asks for | Evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Original proposal, section 02](https://millenniumproblems.bio/) | Recovery of intact adult wild-type mice after at least 24 hours frozen or vitrified, with viability above 99% and no permanent damage | Whole-animal challenge; not an isolated-organ endpoint |
| [Han et al., Nature Communications (2023), DOI 10.1038/s41467-023-38824-8](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-38824-8) | The abstract reports rat kidneys stored for up to 100 days, recovered, and transplanted with life-sustaining renal function | The preserved material was an organ; the recipient was not itself cryopreserved |

The paper supplies a relevant organ-level result. It does not demonstrate whole-animal recovery or establish a solution to the proposed challenge. This finding does not claim that no other relevant evidence exists.

## How to check

Open section 02 of the proposal and the paper's title and abstract. Compare organism, preserved material, storage interval and recovery endpoint. No methods reproduction, sample-level reanalysis, dataset download or license audit was performed. Source retrieval succeeded through the abstract; this note does not claim review of the entire paper. Linked material retains its own rights.
